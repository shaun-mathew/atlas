# Version learner-facing geographic facts

Fact cards use reviewed structured facts with source, retrieval date, reference year, and geographic scope, and each presented fact is versioned. Population direction is informational rather than an initial test skill. Versioning preserves the meaning of historical answers when populations, names, boundaries, or other geographic facts change, instead of silently rewriting learner history.

Published country, city, and water-feature releases are registered oldest to newest in `facts.ts`, `cities.ts`, and `water.ts`; retain old bundles and release IDs, and add a reviewed snapshot or static overlay rather than editing published content. Every entity requires geographic scope and a reference year (explicitly unavailable when the source does not establish one), and every source requires a retrieval date and license; retrieval and review dates are not substitutes for a fact's reference year. The release catalogue validates this metadata and freezes its snapshots before any fact card can use them; it does not fetch or generate claims at runtime.

Attempts retain their original fact version, grading outcome, and spatial interpretation; reading-only and post-answer card presentations also retain their displayed version and merge by immutable presentation identity. Known nonmaterial releases continue contributing to proficiency, while unavailable historical releases remain in durable history without contributing to current assessment. A reviewed material change explicitly names the affected entity, assessed skills, and reason: it advances only those learning items' next reviews, retaining scheduler history; an immediate retry or practice answer cannot discharge that review, and population direction is never an assessed skill.

Historical country feedback is shown only when its boundary version is available; city feedback uses the versioned canonical point and role. An obsolete city role, country relationship, or assessment tolerance may remain visible in history but cannot become a new retry under the current release.

Water-feature attempts retain their mapped geometry version and selection tolerance alongside the fact version. Rivers use sourced main-stem lines; lakes, seas, and oceans use filled water extents rather than country ownership or drainage basins. The dated water bundle records source components, coastline clipping, curated nonexhaustive country/region relationships, and scale tolerance. A water feature has its own identity and proficiency, never a substitute country ID. Unavailable historical water geometry cannot be reinterpreted against a new outline for feedback or retry.

Lake and ocean extents remain assessment data, but neither the map nor globe draws their quiz fills or target highlights. Location help can still frame them, and answers retain their geographic grading, facts, and review schedule.

Ordinary inland water is separate basemap context: the map, linked country views, and globe draw Natural Earth's 1:50m lakes and reservoirs in the normal water color, preserving islands and keeping water visible through country highlights. This generalized layer includes all five Great Lakes rather than only the curated quiz lakes; it is not an exhaustive inventory of inland waters and never replaces versioned assessment geometry. City satellite views use their imagery instead.
